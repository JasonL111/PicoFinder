# PicoFinder v1.0.0

PicoFinder is a local Bluetooth Low Energy item finder for DIY tags. Use an Android phone
to watch relative signal strength and narrow your search for keys, bags and other nearby items.

## Features

- Discover multiple tags, save local names and calibrate RSSI at 1 metre.
- Smoothed RSSI, approximate distance, approach/away trends and offline detection.
- A calm signal ring, light/dark themes, English and Simplified Chinese interfaces.
- One brief vibration only after a sustained, very strong signal. Thresholds remain fixed
  at −45 dBm to enter and −51 dBm to rearm; the alert can be switched off per tag.
- The supplied concentric-circle artwork is adapted as an Android launcher icon.
- Foreground scanning only, limited to two minutes per session. No account, server or Internet connection.

## Requirements and installation

Android 12 / API 31 or newer, with BLE support. Download `picofinder-1.0.0.apk` from this
release, allow installation from your browser/file manager when prompted, then open PicoFinder.
Grant Nearby devices and precise location permissions and enable Bluetooth and system Location.
The app estimates relative BLE distance without reading GPS coordinates.

The tag uses a Raspberry Pi **Pico 2 W** with official **RPI_PICO2_W MicroPython 1.27.0**,
powered over USB. Existing PicoFinder firmware and the v1 broadcast protocol are unchanged;
no reflash, pairing or GATT connection is required for this Android update.

## Known limits and validation

RSSI is not precise ranging. Bodies, furniture, walls, orientation and reflections affect it.
PicoFinder cannot identify a direction or room, and a missing signal does not prove an item is outdoors.
It does not join Google Find Hub and provides no GPS, crowdsourced network, maps or theft protection.

The user reported a working Pico 2 W / Android link, trends at 0.5 m, 1 m and 3 m, unplug/offline
detection and a stable ID after reboot. This is limited manual feedback, not certification of every
phone, radio environment or physical vibration setting. Automated and hardware validation are
recorded separately in `docs/testing.md`.

The formal APK uses a maintainer-controlled signing key. Old development APKs used ephemeral Debug
keys and may not accept an in-place upgrade. Removing an old app erases its local data, so record
names and calibration parameters before any uninstall. Future formal updates require the same signing key.

See the English and Chinese READMEs for firmware deployment, calibration and nearby-finding guidance.
